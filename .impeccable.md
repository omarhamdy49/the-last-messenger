## Design Context

### Users
Content editors, translators, and administrators managing an Arabic-first Islamic encyclopedia. They need to review large canonical datasets, find records quickly, update localized presentation content, and control publication without compromising source identity or hierarchy.

### Brand Personality
Match the existing admin modules: authoritative, polished, and efficient. The interface should inspire confidence and care around religious source material while remaining practical for daily editorial work.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use the existing admin design system without introducing a separate visual language: Cairo and Amiri typography, blue/green brand colors with teal accents, light and dark themes, responsive RTL/LTR layouts, Lucide icons, Alpine interactions, and established Blade components.

### Design Principles
- Reuse existing admin components, tokens, spacing, and interaction patterns.
- Keep data-heavy workflows scannable through clear hierarchy and progressive disclosure.
- Treat canonical Hadith identity and hierarchy as protected source data.
- Make publication state, filters, validation, and destructive consequences explicit.
- Preserve full keyboard, responsive, dark-mode, and RTL/LTR usability.
